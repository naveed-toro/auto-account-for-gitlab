# Privacy policy

*Auto Account for GitLab — last updated November 18, 2026*

The short version: everything the extension knows stays in your browser, on your computer. Nothing is sent to me or to anyone else.

## What it stores

The extension keeps this in your browser's local extension storage:

- **Your saved accounts.** For each account you save: its gitlab.com cookies (this is what keeps you signed in), its username, and when it was saved.
- **Which group opens with which account.** The first part of a gitlab.com address (a group or user name, like `my-company`) and the account that can open it.
- **Which account is in use right now.**

## What it reads

- Cookies for **gitlab.com only**, to save an account and to switch between accounts.
- The addresses of **gitlab.com pages only** as they load, to see which group a link belongs to and whether GitLab answered with a 404.

It does not read any other site, and it can't see the addresses of your other tabs.

## Who it talks to

Only gitlab.com itself, to ask which account is signed in. There are no other servers, no analytics, and no tracking. I have no server that receives anything from the extension.

## Sharing

Nothing is shared, sold or transferred to anyone.

## Security

Saved sessions are stored unencrypted, just like the browser's own cookies. Anyone who can use your browser profile can use them, so don't install the extension on a shared computer.

## Deleting your data

- Click **✕** next to an account in the extension menu to delete that account, its saved cookies and its rules.
- Uninstall the extension to delete everything it stored.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published here, with a new date at the top.
